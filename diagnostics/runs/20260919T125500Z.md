## Decision

Design A REJECTED. Design B adopted as principle, **ordered behind provenance**,
**executed as retirement rather than deletion**. Written up in
`docs/DETECTOR_ADMISSION.md`.

The boundary rule: *the detector exists to cover the predicate vocabulary's
blind spots, not to mirror the corpus.* Per-condition, not per-chapter — so no
chapter ever needs a tagging decision and no third hierarchy appears.

## The finding that reordered the design

Validation Source's HIGH, confirmed by measurement and worse than estimated:
**47 of 144 detector rows cite no sloka** — every kendra-trikona raja link, all
five Pancha Mahapurusha, all seven Neecha Bhanga, all three Vipareeta links,
Gajakesari, Adhi, Vesi, Nipuna, Kalpadruma, Yogada, the sankhya Naabhasa set.

A predicate-redundancy admission test filters on decidability, not provenance —
so it would have **retained all 47 unsourced rows while retiring 19 correctly
cited ones.** Gate order is therefore source-named first, redundancy second.

## One roster recommendation NOT followed

## Live proof carried into the registry

the 4th. Detector resolved to the Sanskrit. In capture turn 2 the interpreter

and corpus disagree in production, the interpreter follows the corpus, and the
mis-evaluation landed in `silent_on` where nothing checks it. That single
observation is both the registry's justification and P-033's evidence.
