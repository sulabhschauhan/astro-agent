  it restates from the other side). Turn 2 has only 2 claims, both

- **Did it use the verified/unverified distinction at all?** Yes, explicitly
  and consistently — every unverified claim in both turns is flagged in its
  own sentence ("was not verified," "wasn't verified against your chart,"
  "not fully verified"), and turn 1's lead sentence states the 2-verified/
  4-unverified split up front. Turn 2's lead states "both key points are
  unverified."
- **Any jargon that survived?** One instance: turn 1's rewrites use
  "angle–trine" repeatedly (5 of 6 claim blocks plus the lead and the
  closing note) as a shorthand for kendra–trikona — never glossed in plain
  words, unlike the system prompt's own banned-jargon list example

  debilitated`). "Rajayoga" from the original claim text does NOT survive —
  it is correctly translated to "a rise-giving combination" / "yoga for
  rise" is dropped for plain phrasing. No other listed jargon term

## Composer-off vs composer-on shape, at a glance

The current (composer-off) answer is a flat bullet list of the interpreter's
own claim sentences, each carrying its raw citation markers and no verdict
framing — the reader has to infer from the prose whether a claim is settled
or conditional. The composed answer adds: (1) a lead sentence stating the
